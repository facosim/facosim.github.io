Flight Instruments

The map shows the aircraft over a real US area: the airports with instrument approaches, their localizers, and the VORs, VORTACs, TACANs, DMEs and NDBs around them, from FAA data. Pick a home airport (type its code, such as KDAB or KOSH) and an approach at the top; the scenarios are built around it and its nearest VOR, TACAN and NDB. The panel shows what the pilot sees. Pick a scenario and the autopilot flies it; touch any control and you have the aircraft.

Controls

  • Fly: the Bank, Pitch and Power sliders, or ← → bank, ↑ ↓ pitch, W S power, L wings level. Hold Shift for finer steps.
  • Steer with the mouse: press and hold on the aircraft and drag, like a stick. Left or right picks a new heading (5° for every 10 px, Shift for 1° steps) and the aircraft rolls into a standard-rate turn (3° a second) to fly it. Up climbs and down descends, 100 ft for every 4 px, at 500 fpm, the way Briefing Lines sets climb by dragging its ticks. The blue line ahead shows the planned path, with Briefing Lines' climb ticks across its middle: one tick level, two a gentle climb or descent, three a steep one, growing to climb and shrinking to descend. It uses the heading bug and the HDG and ALT holds, so the instruments show where you are going; let go and the aircraft finishes the turn and levels off by itself. Drag again mid-turn to add to it.
  • Quick menu: right-click the aircraft (press and hold on a touchscreen) for a ring of quick changes laid out like the drag: climb or descend 500 ft, turn 30° or 90° either way, and 10 kt faster or slower (which holds the speed with power). Clicks add up, and Shift+click doubles a step. The middle shows the heading, altitude and speed, blue where they are being held. Below it: Level off, Turn 180°, Hold speed, Hand-fly (autopilot off) and Move. Click outside or press Esc to close.
  • Map: Shift+drag the aircraft (or Move in the quick menu) to move it, and drag the yellow dot ahead of it to point the nose (Shift snaps to 15°). Drag empty map to pan, wheel or pinch to zoom, Follow (F) keeps the aircraft centered, Fit (0) shows everything.
  • Autopilot: set the heading bug with − and + (, .) and press HDG (H) to fly it; ALT (A) holds the current altitude; NAV (N) tracks the OBS course through the VOR, or the localizer and glideslope with the ILS selected. Moving the bank slider releases HDG and NAV, moving pitch releases ALT, and moving power releases the speed hold.
  • Radios: type a frequency in NAV1 (a VOR such as 112.60, or a localizer such as 109.70), a channel in TACAN (such as 73X), or a kHz in ADF, or pick a station from Tune nearby. NAV1 or TCN (V) picks which one drives the nav indicator; − and + ([ ]) turn the OBS. On the map the tuned station's compass rose is turned to its magnetic north, the selected course is the dashed blue line and the thin orange line is the radial you are on.
  • What each station gives: a VOR gives a radial; VOR/DME and VORTAC add DME on NAV1. The aircraft has a military TACAN receiver, so a TACAN or VORTAC gives a radial and DME by channel. A standalone TACAN tuned on NAV1 (its paired VHF frequency) gives DME only, as in a civil aircraft. A DME gives distance only. An ILS gives the localizer and glideslope, and DME where the approach has it. An NDB gives a bearing on the ADF. A station only comes in within its published service volume (25 to 130 nm by class and height) and radio line of sight; out of range the flag shows.
  • Magnetic: headings, courses, radials and bearings are magnetic, like in the airplane. Each VOR's radials use that station's own variation, so they can differ by a few degrees from the area's. The wind is in degrees true, like a winds-aloft forecast.
  • Panel: Six-pack or Glass (G) switches between the analog gauges and a G1000-style glass cockpit. Both show the same aircraft, so you can switch mid-flight and compare.
  • Scenarios: Play (Space) pauses and resumes, ↺ Restart starts the scenario over, 1× 2× 4× sets the speed. The status bar says what the autopilot is doing.

The instruments

  • Airspeed: indicated airspeed in knots. White arc: flap range. Green: normal. Yellow: smooth air only. Red line: never exceed.
  • Attitude: blue sky over brown ground. The orange airplane is you; the horizon tilts with bank and drops as the nose rises. Pitch lines every 5°, bank marks at 10, 20, 30, 45 and 60°.
  • Altimeter: the long hand reads hundreds, the short wide hand thousands, the thin hand with the triangle tens of thousands. The window shows the altimeter setting, fixed at 29.92.
  • Turn coordinator: the airplane banks to show the rate of turn. Wings on the lower marks mean a standard rate of 3° per second, a full circle in two minutes. The ball shows coordination (the model always flies coordinated).
  • Heading indicator: the card turns so your heading is under the line at the top. The orange bug is the autopilot's heading.
  • Vertical speed: climb or descent in hundreds of feet per minute.
  • ADF: a fixed card with 0 at the nose: the needle points at the NDB, so it shows the relative bearing. Magnetic bearing to the station = heading + relative bearing. With no signal the needle parks at 90°. On the glass cockpit the cyan BRG1 pointer on the HSI points at the NDB against the compass rose, and the box on the left gives the magnetic bearing.
  • VOR/ILS: turn the OBS so the course is at the top. The needle shows which side the course is on: each dot is 2° for a VOR and ½° for a localizer. With a VOR the triangle says whether that course takes you TO or FROM the station, and the OFF flag shows out of range or right over it. With the ILS the horizontal needle is the glideslope (each dot 0.14°): needle below center means fly down. Flying away from the runway on a back course the needle reads backwards.
  • Glass cockpit: the same information on one screen. The attitude fills the background with the yellow aircraft symbol in the middle; airspeed is the tape on the left (TAS below it), altitude on the right with vertical speed beside it, the selected altitude in cyan above and the altimeter setting below. Magenta bars on the tapes show where airspeed and altitude will be in 6 seconds. The HSI at the bottom combines the heading indicator and the nav indicator: cyan is the heading bug, the green arrow is the course with the deviation bar in its middle (2 dots each side, full scale), the small green triangle points TO or FROM, the magenta diamond is the ground track and the magenta arc on top is where the heading will be in 6 seconds. With the ILS the green diamond left of the altitude tape is the glideslope. The top bar shows the nav frequencies (green is the one feeding the HSI) and the autopilot modes, active in green and armed in white; the boxes beside the HSI show the wind and DME.
  • Readout: the lower right box lists the NAV1 frequency and TACAN channel with the station they hear (green drives the nav indicator), the course, the radial you are on, DME distance and station, the flags, the ADF station and bearing, heading and bug, ground track and groundspeed, and the wind.

Data

  • Navaids and approaches: FAA NASR, cycle (public domain, via the @squawk data packages). No terrain: the ground is the home airport's elevation. For training only, not for navigation.
