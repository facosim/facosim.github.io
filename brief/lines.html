Controls and layout

Where things are

  • Top bar: add and remove aircraft, the mouse tools (Select, Path, Pan, Zoom), zoom and Fit, the 3D view and Advanced windows, and on the right Touch, ↺ Reset, help and settings. When it is wider than the screen it scrolls sideways.
  • Bottom timeline: previous and next frame, Play and the scrub slider for the upcoming frame, the current frame's length, and Clear later frames.
  • Right: the selected aircraft: name and color, where the frame starts, the controls for the next frame, the vertical window and maneuvers, and its turn numbers. The vertical window opens beside it.
  • Left: the Advanced window (radar and debrief markers) at the top and the 3D view at the bottom. Drag any window by its title to move it.

Controls

  • Add: pick a type, press + Add (A), click the grid and drag to point the nose. Shift snaps heading to 15°. The named aircraft, from the T-6 trainer to the J-20, are drawn with their own top-down outline and carry public performance figures (stall and corner speed, sea-level top speed, g limit, thrust and drag), all editable on the Aircraft tab of Settings (⚙). ↺ Reset (top right) clears the whole debrief.
  • Shape the flight path: click an aircraft and drag. The next frame's arc bends through the cursor, and g and bank are set to match. The Flight path tool (F) does the same from anywhere on the grid.
  • Turn or move: Option, Ctrl or ⌘ + drag points the nose at the cursor; the yellow handle does the same. Shift + drag moves the aircraft. After an aircraft's first frame these are locked to how it flew; press Unlock in the panel to correct a frame by hand.
  • Name and color: edit them at the top of the aircraft panel. A new color applies from the current frame on, so earlier frames keep the color they had. Delete removes the selected aircraft.
  • Climb and descent: lift tilts with bank. With wings level, more than 1 g pulls the nose up. In a bank, lift that doesn't hold the aircraft up lets it descend, and past 90° of bank the aircraft is reversing: it keeps pulling through the vertical until the nose is back on the horizon, then rolls wings level heading the opposite way (Split-S when rolled inverted, Immelmann when pulled over the top). From above, a pure Split-S or Immelmann draws out and back along the same line, so watch the altitude and the ticks. Hold altitude sets the bank that keeps the current climb angle at the current g, and dragging the flight path does the same automatically.
  • Vertical ticks: across the middle of each frame's path. The first tick is the reference. One tick means level, two mean a gentle climb or descent, and three mean a steep one. Ticks that grow mean climbing and ticks that shrink mean descending, and a bigger size step means more altitude change over the frame.
  • Vertical window: click the tick marks in the middle of the upcoming frame's path (or Vertical… in the aircraft panel) to edit its altitude, climb angle, g and bank, and to start a reversal. Past frames' ticks are display only.
  • Maneuvers: the Maneuver… menu in the aircraft panel sets the selected aircraft's flight path to a Split-S, Immelmann, Pitchback or Sliceback starting this frame; the whole maneuver is previewed until it rolls out.
  • Reversals: Split-S, Immelmann, Pitchback (45° bank, nose high) and Sliceback (135° bank, nose low) roll and pull until the aircraft is level and heading the other way.
  • Climb angle: drag the upcoming frame's tick marks up or down to set the climb angle it starts with.
  • Frame data: hover a past frame's position to see its altitude, airspeed, heading, climb, g, bank and throttle.
  • 3D view: with an aircraft selected, the 3D card (bottom left, 3D view button to show or hide) shows a simple model rolled and pitched to its attitude, with the lift vector and g. Press ▶ or drag its slider (or use the timeline) to play through the upcoming frame (the aircraft moves along its path on the map), and drag the picture to look around.
  • Advanced: the Advanced button opens a window with a radar sector (a 120° field of view ahead of the selected aircraft at 5, 10, 20 or 40 nm, with a contact table of bearing, range, aspect, closure and altitude difference) and debrief markers: missile shots that fly toward a chosen target over a set time of flight, gun bursts drawn ahead of the nose, and flares dropped one by one along the path flown during the next frame. Markers sit on the frame where you add them; hover one on the map for its details.
  • Throttle: W/S or Option+wheel. G: Shift+wheel. Roll: Q/E (up to 160°). Speedbrake: B.
  • Frames: → next, ← previous. Each frame flies every aircraft for the frame time with the settings shown for that frame. The Frame length box in the timeline sets how long the current frame lasts, to the next one; each frame can differ, and frames you don't set follow the default in settings. Clear later frames deletes the frames after the current one.
  • Scrub: the timeline slider, or Play (P), slides every aircraft along its path through the upcoming frame, with the time in yellow; missile markers and flares move with it. The 3D view's slider is the same control. Stepping to another frame puts it back to the start.
  • View: wheel or pinch to zoom, Space+drag, middle drag or drag empty space to pan, 0 fits everything.

Touchscreen

  • Touch in the top bar (or the General tab of Settings) switches touchscreen mode. It starts on by itself on phones and tablets and gives bigger buttons and slider handles.
  • Map: one finger on empty map pans, two fingers pinch to zoom and pan. Tap an aircraft to select it, tap empty map to deselect.
  • Drag from an aircraft does what the panel's Drag buttons say: Shape path bends the next frame's turn, Point nose and Move replace Option+drag and Shift+drag (first frame, or after Unlock).
  • Tap a past frame's symbol or a marker for its data, and tap the mid-frame ticks to open the vertical window.
  • Windows: drag a title to move it and double-tap the title to put it back. On a phone the aircraft panel sits above the timeline, ▾ folds it to its title, and one window shows at a time.

Flight model

Level turn: radius = V² / (g·√(n²−1)), rate = g·√(n²−1) / V. Available g is limited by lift, (V / stall speed)², up to the type's max g, so corner speed = stall speed × √(max g). Speed change per second = thrust × throttle − parasite drag (grows with V²) − induced drag (grows with g², falls with V²) − speedbrake drag. Lift n tilted by bank φ gives a turning part n·sin φ and a vertical part n·cos φ − cos γ, which bends the climb angle γ at g·(n·cos φ − cos γ) / V. Climbing trades speed for height: g·sin γ comes off the speed each second. The aircraft is flown in 3D, so it can pass through the vertical; the pilot holds g and bank through each frame without rolling.
